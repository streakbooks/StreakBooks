# Settings benchmark (clean-room notes)

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-26  
**Method:** official public product help, user guides and public developer documentation only. No account access, authenticated pages, competitor assets, screenshots, source templates or code were used. These notes describe general product patterns in our own words; they are requirements research, not a screen specification to copy.

## Product comparison

| Product | Publicly documented pattern | Useful product lesson | Limit of this evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xero | Organisation settings include organisation details and financial settings; users and permissions are managed within organisation settings. Some accounting settings are reachable from the Accounting area. Roles and history are described explicitly. | Keep organisation-wide financial configuration near accounting work, explain role boundaries, and make settings changes traceable. | The help centre documents destinations and tasks, not every current layout variant or responsive behavior. |
| QuickBooks Online | A gear menu offers a dedicated Account and settings area, while user management, billing, audit and other tools remain adjacent destinations. Account settings are grouped into tabs/sections and edited with clear save/done actions. | Distinguish the account-wide configuration hub from the module workspaces; make sectioned edits reviewable and save explicitly. | Tab structure varies by edition/region and public guides do not establish one universal UI. |
| Zoho Books | Settings is a broad organization setup area. Users and Roles has a dedicated home; role editing can be module-aware and, in some plans, access can be segmented by locations, tags or accounts. | Separate access administration from personal profile; show role scope and data boundaries clearly. | Feature availability differs by country, plan and organization configuration. |
| FreshBooks | Settings and billing are distinct from the primary business workspaces. Team role permissions differ by module; some staff can be limited to selected clients. Personal/business settings may be presented through different entry points. | Make role-dependent controls visible and describe client scope before inviting people. | This source is a detailed permission article, not an exhaustive settings IA specification. |
| Wave | Business settings contains user management and roles. A collaborator can be invited to each business separately, and the role table explains module-level access. | Scope collaborators to an explicit business and explain what each role can view or change. | Features and collaborator roles depend on the current plan and region. |
| MYOB Business | Getting-started material frames initial setup around tax, business details, fiscal year, categories and linked categories. Users and an accounting advisor are separately invited with roles and permissions. | Setup should lead owners through high-impact finance and tax basics, then guide collaboration. | MYOB has multiple product lines; this note covers MYOB Business help, not all MYOB interfaces. |
| Sage Accounting | Public setup guidance separates business preferences, document settings, financial settings, user management, data management, app connections and opening balances. User roles can vary by product. | Organize configuration by the decision being made, while making product-specific permissions and access limits legible. | Country editions and Sage product families have different role and settings options. |
| Odoo | General Settings is the administrative app; user access rights are configured on user records and per application, while portal access is a separate external-user role. Multi-company access is explicit. The public `odoo/odoo` repository license declares LGPLv3. | Separate internal-user, administrator and portal access; make company scope explicit and avoid a single ambiguous “admin” toggle. | Odoo’s module-based ERP model is broader than SMB accounting software. LGPLv3 is not one of the permissive dependency licenses approved by this task; no Odoo code or dependency is used. |

## Cross-product observations

1. Settings are available from a persistent application shell or a predictable account/company menu, but module-specific setup is often linked from the module that uses it.
2. Organization configuration, user access and subscription management are related but are not interchangeable scopes. User profile controls are personal, while company identity and financial defaults belong to an organisation.
3. Permissions are presented as a role plus an explanation of effective access; several products constrain access by module, client, business or other data segments.
4. Accounting settings are treated as behavior-changing controls. Defaults such as currency, period, tax treatment, time zone and lock dates deserve contextual explanations and careful confirmation.
5. Public help material regularly describes explicit Save, Update or Done actions and role requirements. It does not support the assumption that every product uses one universal save-bar design.
6. Plan and country differences matter. A control should not imply universal availability where a feature depends on plan, region, account type or a connected provider.
7. The public sources do not provide reliable evidence for cross-product mobile interactions, breadcrumb patterns, keyboard behavior or unsaved-change guards. StreakBooks should define and test those directly against its own acceptance contract.
